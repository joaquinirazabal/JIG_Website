Joaquín Irazábal González

I am an industrial engineer with a PhD in Structural Analysis. I use numerical simulation and machine learning to understand how materials, structures and industrial processes behave — from railway ballast and granular materials to dams and metal forming. I am an Assistant Research Professor at CIMNE in Barcelona, in the Machine Learning in Civil Engineering group.

My work sits between physics-based models and data: calibrating simulations with machine-learning surrogates, interpreting dam monitoring records, and optimising forming processes. I also contribute to scientific software in Kratos Multiphysics.

About me

Trained as an industrial engineer, I completed a PhD in Structural Analysis at Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya on the discrete element modelling of railway ballast, which received the XVI Talgo Award for Technological Innovation. Since 2019 my work has focused on applying machine learning to industrial engineering and dam safety: surrogate models for process optimisation, automatic calibration of numerical models, and hybrid approaches for monitoring and anomaly detection.

I take part in research on flowforming and metal forming, shear-link dissipators and dam monitoring, and I co-develop SOLDIER for dam behaviour interpretation. Granular materials and railway infrastructure were the focus of my earlier research and remain part of my technical background.

What I work on

Research in detail →
  • Machine learning for industrial engineering. Surrogate models and simulation-based optimisation for flowforming, rotary metal forming and resilient structural devices.
  • Dam safety and monitoring. Data-driven and hybrid models for interpreting monitoring records, detecting anomalies and defining warning thresholds.
  • Numerical modelling and scientific software. Finite element and particle methods, automatic model calibration and open-source research software, including earlier work on granular materials and railway infrastructure.
