What I work on

Research

My research combines computational mechanics, numerical simulation and machine learning. The main application areas are industrial engineering and dam safety; my earlier work focused on granular materials and railway infrastructure.

01

Machine learning for industrial engineering

I apply machine learning and numerical simulation to industrial forming processes and structural devices. In flowforming and rotary metal forming, the aim is to optimise preforms and process parameters while reducing the cost of physical trials and simulation campaigns. I develop surrogate models and automatic calibration strategies that connect finite element simulations with data-driven predictions. I also work on the numerical design and assessment of shear-link dissipators for resilient buildings.

Representative work: Advanced modeling of buckling delayed shear links · Related projects: OPTIFLOW · OPTIPRO · OPTIREP · RESILINK

02

Dam safety and monitoring

Dams generate long, multivariate monitoring records that are difficult to interpret with classical methods. I work on data-driven and hybrid approaches that combine finite element models with machine learning to detect anomalies, predict displacements and define warning thresholds. Recent work includes autoencoder-based outlier detection, kernel-density-based adaptive thresholds and boosted regression trees for interpreting dam response.

Representative work: Detection of outliers with autoencoders · Adaptive warning thresholds · SOLDIER · Hybrid prediction of arch dam deformations

03

Machine learning for numerical models

Numerical models depend on parameters that are costly to calibrate. I develop surrogate-based and automatic calibration methodologies that replace expensive simulations with machine learning models, and I study how to interpret the resulting predictions. This work supports both industrial process models and dam safety applications.

Representative work: Calibration of DEM parameters with machine learning surrogates

04

Scientific software and digital tools

I contribute to the development of research software in C++ and Python, including SOLDIER for dam behaviour interpretation and EasyHydro for hydrodynamic modelling of dams and reversible hydropower plants. I have also coordinated code development tasks in collaborative research projects.

05

Granular materials and railway infrastructure Earlier research

Earlier in my career I studied the mechanical behaviour of granular materials using the Discrete Element Method, with a focus on particle shape, rolling friction and contact integration. Applications included railway ballast under high-speed train loads, rockfill and rockfall protection systems. This line provided the methodological base for my later work on surrogate modelling and parameter calibration.

Representative work: Bounded rolling friction model for railway ballast · Integration scheme for non-spherical particles · Double Hierarchy Method · Round robin test on angle of repose
