What I work on

Research

My research sits between computational mechanics and data-driven modelling. I develop numerical methods, calibrate them with machine learning, and apply the resulting models to monitoring and safety problems in civil engineering.

01

Dam safety and monitoring

Dams generate long, multivariate monitoring records that are difficult to interpret with classical methods. I work on data-driven and hybrid approaches that combine finite element models with machine learning to detect anomalies, predict displacements and define warning thresholds. Recent work includes autoencoder-based outlier detection, kernel-density-based adaptive thresholds and boosted regression trees for interpreting dam response.

Representative work: Detection of outliers with autoencoders · Adaptive warning thresholds · SOLDIER · Hybrid prediction of arch dam deformations

02

Discrete element modelling of granular materials

I study the mechanical behaviour of granular materials using the Discrete Element Method, with a focus on particle shape, rolling friction and contact integration. Applications include railway ballast under high-speed train loads, rockfill and rockfall protection systems. I also work on DEM–FEM coupling for particle–structure interaction and on the geometric representation of real particle morphologies.

Representative work: Bounded rolling friction model for railway ballast · Integration scheme for non-spherical particles · Double Hierarchy Method · Round robin test on angle of repose

03

Machine learning for numerical models

Numerical models depend on parameters that are costly to calibrate. I develop surrogate-based and automatic calibration methodologies that replace expensive simulations with machine learning models, and I study how to interpret the resulting predictions. This work connects DEM parameter identification with hybrid modelling of dam behaviour.

Representative work: Calibration of DEM parameters with machine learning surrogates · Hybrid approaches for dam displacement prediction

04

Scientific software and digital tools

I contribute to the development of research software in C++ and Python, including SOLDIER for dam behaviour interpretation and EasyHydro for hydrodynamic modelling of dams and reversible hydropower plants. I have also coordinated code development tasks in collaborative research projects.
